Subhas Chandra Sarkar v. State of West Bengal (2026 INSC 1052): Supreme Court Says a Victim's Appeal Against a Magistrate's Acquittal Goes to the Sessions Court, Not the High Court

By Adv. Vikram Panwar · District Courts, Charkhi Dadri & Bhiwani, Haryana

Court / Leading CaseSupreme Court of India — Subhas Chandra Sarkar v. The State of West Bengal & Ors.
BenchJustice J.B. Pardiwala and Justice K. Vinod Chandran
Date22 September 2026
Citation2026 INSC 1052; 2026 LiveLaw (SC) 990
Key SectionsSection 413 (proviso) and Section 415 BNSS, 2023; Section 304A IPC / Section 106(1) BNS

What the case was about

The appellant's son died by suicide while admitted in a hospital. The father lodged a complaint alleging medical negligence against a doctor and a nurse, and the case was tried by a Judicial Magistrate at Alipore for the offence of causing death by negligence under Section 304A IPC. The Magistrate acquitted the accused.

The father, as the victim's family, wished to challenge the acquittal. He filed an appeal directly before the Calcutta High Court. The High Court held that the appeal was not maintainable before it and told him to approach the Sessions Court. He then came to the Supreme Court, which had to decide a short but practically important question: after the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, which forum hears a victim's appeal against an acquittal recorded by a Magistrate?

What the court held

The proviso to Section 413 BNSS gives every victim a right to appeal against an order of acquittal, against conviction for a lesser offence, or against inadequate compensation. The Court focused on the words of that proviso: the appeal must be filed before the court to which an appeal ordinarily lies against an order of conviction passed by the trial court in question.

That left the answer to Section 415 BNSS, which sets out where convictions are appealed. A conviction by a Magistrate (First or Second Class) is appealable to the Court of Session. So, the Bench reasoned, a victim's appeal against a Magistrate's acquittal must also go to the Sessions Court, and not straight to the High Court. The Court described this as a slight deviation in the BNSS from the position under the old Code, where the victim's proviso to Section 372 CrPC was read with less clarity about the forum.

The Court upheld the High Court's view on maintainability. It left the door open for the father: if he still wished to appeal, he could approach the jurisdictional Sessions Court. Published summaries differ slightly on the mechanics (one records the special leave petition as dismissed with liberty to approach the Sessions Court; another records a one-month window with delay excused), so a practitioner relying on the time protection should read the operative paragraph of the judgment itself.

Why this matters for Criminal Law practitioners in Haryana

Every complainant who loses a cheque-bounce, negligence, hurt or other Magistrate-tried case now has a clear forum for a victim's appeal: the Sessions Court at the district headquarters, that is Charkhi Dadri, Bhiwani or Rohtak, depending on the sessions division. Filing in the High Court at Chandigarh will only cost time, and limitation runs meanwhile. Counsel for complainants should check the date of the acquittal and file in the correct forum without delay, since condonation cannot be assumed.

For the defence, the ruling means that the risk of a challenge to an acquittal from a Magistrate's court sits with the Sessions Judge, and the reasoning is the same as that used for convictions. Accused persons who were acquitted should watch for a victim's appeal being served on them at the Sessions level, and should be ready to defend the acquittal on the merits.

Two cautions. First, this judgment deals with acquittals by a Magistrate. Where the trial court is a Sessions Court, the same logic points to the High Court, because that is where a conviction by a Sessions Judge is appealed under Section 415(2) BNSS; but that extension is my reading of the reasoning, not a holding I have seen stated in the sources. Second, an appeal by the State against an acquittal, and an appeal by a complainant in a complaint case requiring special leave, follow Section 419 BNSS, which remains a separate track. The victim's right under Section 413 proviso does not require leave, which is a real advantage over the complainant's route.

Statutory cross-references

Section 413 BNSS (no appeal except as provided; victim's proviso) corresponds to Section 372 CrPC. Section 415 BNSS (appeals from convictions) corresponds to Section 374 CrPC. Section 419 BNSS (appeal in case of acquittal) corresponds to Section 378 CrPC. Section 304A IPC (death by negligence) corresponds to Section 106(1) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023.
