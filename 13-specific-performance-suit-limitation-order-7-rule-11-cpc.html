Shobha Vasant Bhoir v. Soni: Supreme Court Rejects a 38-Year-Delayed Specific Performance Suit at the Plaint Stage

By Adv. Vikram Panwar · District Courts, Charkhi Dadri & Bhiwani, Haryana

Court / Leading CaseSupreme Court of India — Shobha Vasant Bhoir & Ors. v. Soni @ Vandana Gurumukhdas Jagiasi & Ors.
BenchJustices Sanjay Karol and Nongmeikapam Kotiswar Singh
Date1 July 2026
Citation2026 INSC 664
Key SectionsOrder VII Rule 11(d), Code of Civil Procedure, 1908; Article 54, Limitation Act, 1963

What the case was about

The dispute traced back to a plot of land in Ulhasnagar originally owned by one Tukaram Daji Bhoir, predecessor-in-interest of the appellants. In 1974, the local Development Plan reserved the land, along with others, for public infrastructure, with affected owners to be rehabilitated at alternate sites. The respondents claimed that in 1984 they had entered into an unregistered agreement to sell the very same property with Bhoir, and that a Power of Attorney had been executed in favour of one of them. In 1988, physical possession of the land was handed over to the authorities to construct the Hill Line Police Station.

Nothing happened for over three decades. In 2002, Bhoir's successors applied for permission to construct on the plots, only to be told that the land had already been earmarked for the police station since 1988. A revision application followed, and eventually the authorities directed the family to identify an alternate site in lieu of the reserved land. It was only in 2018 — thirty-four years after the alleged 1984 agreement — that the respondents wrote to the authorities themselves, asking that the alternate site be allotted to them instead. That claim was rejected, as was their subsequent appeal. While their writ petition was still pending, the appellants went ahead and executed registered sale deeds over the alternate properties allotted to them. Only then, in 2022, did the respondents file a full civil suit seeking specific performance of the 1984 agreement — a suit filed thirty-eight years after the agreement it sought to enforce.

The appellants moved twice under Order VII Rule 11(d) of the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, asking the trial court to reject the plaint outright on the ground that the suit was hopelessly time-barred on its own averments. Both the Civil Judge and, on revision, the Bombay High Court declined to do so, reasoning in effect that the question of limitation needed a trial. The matter reached the Supreme Court.

What the court held

The Supreme Court reversed both courts below and ordered the plaint rejected. The Bench reiterated the settled purpose of Order VII Rule 11(d): to weed out, at the threshold, suits that are barred by law on a plain reading of the plaint, so that courts are not made to sit through years of trial on a claim that could never have succeeded. The plaint's own averments, the Court said, must be read as a whole to test whether the bar applies — and here, the foundation of the respondents' case was squarely the 1984 unregistered agreement.

Article 54 of the Limitation Act, 1963 fixes a three-year limitation period for suits seeking specific performance, running from the date fixed for performance, or, if no date is fixed, from when the plaintiff has notice that performance is refused. The Court found no explanation whatsoever for why the respondents waited more than three decades to sue for a Conveyance Deed. It also rejected the argument that limitation should be treated as running afresh from the date of the High Court's later order in the writ proceedings, holding that a court's passing observation that no substantive suit had yet been filed cannot itself operate to revive or restart a limitation period long since expired. Citing its own earlier decision in Mukund Bhavan Trust v. Shrimant Chhatrapati Udayan Praje Pratapsinh Maharaj Bhonsle (2024), the Bench reaffirmed that limitation begins to run from the date the right to sue first accrues — not from whichever later date is most convenient for the plaintiff. The Court's language was pointed: a litigant who has remained silent for decades cannot be permitted to file a suit as an afterthought, and doing so here amounted to an abuse of the process of the court.

Why this matters for Civil Law practitioners in Haryana

Suits for specific performance of agreements to sell land are a staple of civil litigation in Charkhi Dadri and Bhiwani, where family land transactions, unregistered agreements, and disputes that simmer for years before anyone approaches a court are common. This judgment gives defendant-side counsel a strong, recent authority to move an Order VII Rule 11(d) application early — at the very first hearing, before written statement or trial — wherever a plaint's own dates show the three-year window under Article 54 has plainly lapsed, without needing to wait for a full trial on limitation as a mixed question of fact and law.

For plaintiff-side counsel, the lesson cuts the other way: any suit built on an old, unregistered agreement to sell must plead, clearly and with dates, why limitation should be treated as extended — part performance, continuous acknowledgment, fraud, or a specific later event that genuinely restarts the clock. Vague reliance on "the dispute was always pending somewhere" will not survive this ruling. Two courts below got this wrong before the Supreme Court corrected the position, so counsel should not assume a trial court's refusal to reject a plaint settles the limitation question.

Statutory cross-references

This is a civil law ruling under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and the Limitation Act, 1963, both of which remain unaffected by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, since the 2023 recodification replaced only the Indian Penal Code and the Code of Criminal Procedure and applies to criminal matters, not civil suits. Order VII Rule 11 CPC and Article 54 of the Limitation Act continue to apply in their existing form to all specific performance suits filed in civil courts across Haryana, including at Charkhi Dadri and Bhiwani.
