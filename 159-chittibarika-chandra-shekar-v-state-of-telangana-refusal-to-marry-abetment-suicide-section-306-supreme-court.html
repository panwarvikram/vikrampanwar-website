Chittibarika Chandra Shekar v. State of Telangana: Supreme Court Says Refusing to Marry a Live-In Partner Is Not, By Itself, Abetment of Suicide Under Section 306 IPC (2026 LiveLaw (SC) 1029)

By Adv. Vikram Panwar · District Courts, Charkhi Dadri & Bhiwani, Haryana

Court / Leading CaseSupreme Court of India — Chittibarika Chandra Shekar & Ors. v. State of Telangana & Anr.
BenchJustice J.B. Pardiwala and Justice K. Vinod Chandran
Date5 October 2026 (reported 8 October 2026)
Citation2026 LiveLaw (SC) 1029
Key SectionsIPC Sections 306, 107 (now BNS Sections 108, 45)

What the case was about

A woman and a man had been in a consensual relationship and had lived together for about three months at his house, with his family’s acceptance. The man later refused to marry her, and she died by suicide. Her sister lodged the FIR. Six people were named as accused under Section 306 of the Indian Penal Code: the man, his parents, his sister, a friend, and the man who was proposed as his sister’s bridegroom. The allegation against the five others was that they had influenced him against the marriage. The accused approached the Telangana High Court, and the matter then reached the Supreme Court.

What the Supreme Court held

The Bench of Justice J.B. Pardiwala and Justice K. Vinod Chandran quashed the proceedings against all six accused. The Court held that a refusal to marry, even when followed by the partner’s suicide, does not by itself amount to abetment under Section 306 IPC. Abetment requires instigation, or some act that pushes the person towards suicide, and in this case the Court found no material showing that any of the accused had instigated the deceased to take her life.

Two points in the reasoning, as reported by Bar & Bench and LiveLaw, stand out. First, the refusal was not made with the intention of driving her to suicide, and the accused could not be said to have known that it would. Second, the Bench noted that people react differently to the same situation, and a break-up that ends in suicide cannot always be treated as abetment. Even if the informant’s version was accepted in full, it showed only a refusal to marry followed by a suicide. That was not enough to put the accused on trial, so letting the case continue would have been an abuse of process.

Why this matters for Criminal practitioners in Haryana

Section 306 cases are filed quickly after a suicide, and family members of the other party are often named along with the main accused. Courts in Haryana regularly see such FIRs after a failed relationship, a broken engagement, or a dispute over dowry or marriage. This ruling gives defence counsel a clear authority at the stage of quashing, discharge or bail: if the prosecution case boils down to a refusal to marry, a quarrel, or a break-up, it does not by itself disclose abetment.

It also helps with the question of the extended family. Parents, siblings and friends are frequently added on a general allegation that they “influenced” the main accused. After this judgment, such an allegation has to be backed by something specific: words or conduct that actually pushed the deceased towards the act. Without it, there is a good ground to seek quashing under Section 528 of the BNSS, or discharge at the framing stage.

For prosecutors and the informant side, the lesson is equally practical. The case has to show more than the end of a relationship. Messages, calls, threats, humiliation or a clear pattern of harassment close in time to the death will matter, because the question is whether there was an intention to push the person into taking her life, or knowledge that it was likely. The judgment is on its own facts, which included a three-month consensual live-in relationship and no instigating act, and it should not be read as shielding conduct that goes beyond a plain refusal.

Statutory cross-references

Section 306 IPC (abetment of suicide) corresponds to Section 108 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, and Section 107 IPC (abetment of a thing, including instigation) corresponds to Section 45 BNS. The inherent power to quash proceedings, earlier under Section 482 of the CrPC, is now exercised under Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023.
